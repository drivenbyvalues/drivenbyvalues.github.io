<div class="highlight-box">
    <h3>Seven Years, Four Platforms — Closing Reflection</h3>
    <p>My last day at Visa was <strong>August 14, 2026</strong>. Looking back at seven years, the thread running through all of it wasn't any single platform — it was the recurring chance to take an idea from 0→1 and then carry it from 1→100 alongside genuinely exceptional teams: <strong>Parsec</strong>, <strong>Tusker</strong>, the <strong>NextGen Merchant Data Platform</strong>, and <strong>ViDA.AI</strong>. Along the way I developed a small reputation for naming things — Insight, Quest, Strive — proof that even inventory, queue, and storage management systems deserved a little personality.</p>
    <ul>
        <li><strong>Parsec</strong> — the self-service PaaS portal that turned ad-hoc, manually-managed big-data infrastructure into a fleet of automated agents, and became the shared foundation every other platform service on the team was built on. <a href="/articles/2020-parsec-self-service-paas-portal/">Read the writeup →</a></li>
        <li><strong>Tusker</strong> — handed a plain Hadoop migration, I somehow convinced people that an elephant named Tusker was the obvious answer. What started as a naming joke became a multi-year effort — technical challenges, late nights, and more persistence than I care to admit — that grew into a foundational, self-supported platform for the entire data organization. <a href="/articles/2021-tusker-core-big-data-distribution/">Read the writeup →</a></li>
        <li><strong>NextGen Merchant Data Platform</strong> — the chance to help rethink how Visa understands and models an increasingly complex payments ecosystem, from first architecture sketch to shipped microservices. <a href="/articles/2023-merchant-data-platform-entity-model/">Read the writeup →</a></li>
        <li><strong>ViDA.AI</strong> — the chapter that brought together everything I'd learned. Working with exceptional partners across Privacy Governance, Legal, the Data Office, and the broader ViDA organization, we asked a simple question: what if governance were designed for the AI era instead of adapted to it? What started as that question became an AI-native governance platform — several dozen intelligent agents, dozens of features, hundreds to thousands of releases across multiple repositories, largely built by leveraging AI to build an AI platform. The work continues: it's now being carried forward into Visa's next-generation risk-agent tool chain.</li>
    </ul>
    <blockquote>
        What I'm proudest of was never the technology — it was the mission behind it: reducing the manual effort on routine reviews so governance professionals could focus on where they create the most value — partnering with teams, understanding context, evaluating risk, discussing controls, identifying mitigations, and helping solve problems. Governance shouldn't just identify issues. It should help the business navigate them confidently and responsibly.
    </blockquote>
    <p>None of it was built alone. I had the privilege of working alongside talented colleagues across DAP, O&amp;I, Cybersecurity, VAS, VCA, the Data Office, Privacy Governance, and Legal — solving hard problems, challenging assumptions, and building things together that would have been impossible individually. Technology evolves and platforms change; the people and partnerships are what endure.</p>
</div>
